The problem

Stabilized isn't the same as still.

Gyro stabilizers remove the big shake. Most of them leave a fine shimmer behind: a few tenths of a pixel of high-frequency wobble from prop vibration, and rolling-shutter jello where the correction is slightly out of step with the sensor. You notice it on a big screen and on calm cruising shots, where the frame should be completely still.

How it works

Five details that add up to still.

DJI cameras record their own orientation up to 2,000 times a second. Stillpoint's job is to use that data at the exact moment each row of pixels was captured, and then check the result against the picture.

01

Gyro timing, done right

Every attitude sample goes onto the video's own timeline: camera clock drift is corrected (the O3 runs at 59.9693 Hz, not 59.94), and so is the Osmo Action 4's picture offset, which changes with exposure. At 200–300 Hz of prop vibration, a millisecond of timing error is enough to turn a correction into extra wobble.

02

Rolling shutter, per row

The sensor reads out top to bottom over about 10 ms. Stillpoint computes a separate rotation for 32 rows of every frame and maps each output pixel exactly through the fisheye lens model on the GPU, with no mesh approximation. That's what removes jello instead of smearing it around.

03

Exposure-aware

At slower shutter speeds each row sees the vibration averaged over its exposure. Stillpoint averages the camera orientation over the same window, so it corrects the motion the sensor actually recorded rather than a single instant of it.

04

Crop-constrained smoothing

The camera path is solved as a convex optimization that keeps every output corner inside the source image. You choose the crop, and you get the smoothest path that fits it, with no black borders and nothing guessed at the edges.

05

A vision check

A second, image-based pass measures the rotation that's still left in low-resolution previews. It folds corrections back in one second at a time, and only where the next measurement confirms they helped. It's used for the O3, where the gyro is band-limited, and off for the Osmo Action 4 at 1 kHz.

+

Measured, not eyeballed

Every change is scored with a no-reference jitter metric that tracks image motion in the rendered video itself: high-frequency RMS, 8–30 Hz roll, jello, single-frame jumps and crop footprint, compared window by window against the raw clip and a Gyroflow render.

What's working

  • Less high-frequency jitter than Gyroflow on all 10 windows, and less micro-jitter on calm cruising frames on all 10 (1.5–7.5× lower).
  • 8–30 Hz vibration is below the raw footage on every window. Gyroflow's renders add 8–30 Hz roll jitter above the raw footage on 6 of the 10.
  • On the Osmo Action 4, fixing the exposure-dependent picture offset (0.92 ms at short shutters) cut jello by nearly two thirds.

What isn't solved yet

  • Our strict internal gate (ten criteria per window) doesn't pass on any window yet. The main failures are occasional single-frame jumps of 1–4 px and more jello than Gyroflow on 3 of 10 windows.
  • Low flights past big near objects (0028, alongside a train) show parallax, which no rotation can fix. There the vision check is blind and Stillpoint matches Gyroflow at best.
  • Osmo Action 4 timing at slow shutters (5–16 ms) isn't validated yet, and there's a small 2–3.5 Hz roll leak in the smoother.

No install

Stabilize a clip
in your browser.

Drop in an original DJI MP4. Stillpoint reads the motion data from the file, solves the path, warps every frame on your GPU and saves a stabilized MP4.

The browser version is Stillpoint's gyro-precise mode: the same per-row gyro path and GPU warp, without the Mac app's vision pass (yet). On DJI_0034 (15–40 s), at the same crop as the Mac engine, it measured 0.76 px of high-frequency jitter, against 5.58 raw, 1.31 for Gyroflow and 0.51 for the Mac app with vision.

Cameras & browsers

Built for DJI footage.

Stillpoint reads the attitude data DJI embeds in the original MP4. Use the untouched file straight from the card, not a re-export.

DJI O3 Air Unit · Avata · O4 Pro

High-rate attitude · per-row rolling shutter

Full support for the O3 and Avata. The O3 records attitude at 2 kHz, and it's the camera the results above were measured on. O4 Pro files open too, but their timing is assumed from the O3 and hasn't been checked on real O4 footage yet. Record with the camera's own stabilization (EIS / RockSteady) off.

Osmo Action 4

1 kHz in 4:3 · 60 Hz in 16:9

For the best result, shoot 4K 4:3 with RockSteady and HorizonSteady off. That's the only mode where the camera records 1 kHz motion data. 16:9 clips carry one orientation per frame and stabilize at a lower quality.

Browsers

WebGPU + WebCodecs
  • Use current Chrome or Edge on a desktop or laptop. Tested in Chrome on an Apple-silicon Mac.
  • Safari 26 on a Mac has everything it needs but hasn't been tested yet. Firefox isn't supported.
  • O3 clips are H.264 and open almost anywhere. Osmo Action 4 and O4 Pro clips are HEVC (H.265) and need hardware HEVC decoding: any Apple-silicon Mac, and most recent Windows GPUs.
  • The app checks your browser when it opens and tells you what's missing.

Also available: the Mac app and the engine

A native macOS app (SwiftUI + Metal, the same per-row warp) and the Python analysis engine, including the evaluation suite behind the numbers above, are in the repository. They need Apple silicon and are built from source. Build instructions →
